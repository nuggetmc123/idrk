# Rago Extract

A VR ragdoll extraction shooter for Unity (Meta Quest + PC VR), in the spirit of Ghost of Tabor and Contractors: Exfil Zone.

Everything (guns, characters, maps, textures, sounds) is generated by code at runtime. There are no imported art files.

## Open it

1. Install **Unity 6000.0.40f1** with **Android Build Support** (+ OpenJDK + Android SDK & NDK) from Unity Hub.
2. Unzip `RagoExtract.zip` first (right-click → **Extract All**). Don't open it from inside the zip.
   Unity Hub → **Add → Add project from disk** → pick the folder that directly contains
   `Assets`, `Packages` and `ProjectSettings`. If Hub says *"not a valid project"*, you picked
   the folder one level too high (or the zip itself).
3. On first open the project sets itself up (Quest player settings, the game scene, the world in the Scene view) and opens the **Rago Extract** tab.
4. Press **Play** to try it. Without a headset you can play with keyboard + mouse.

## The Rago Extract tab (top menu → Rago Extract)

Three buttons:

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Set Up VR** | Switches on OpenXR for Quest (Android) and PC VR, with Meta Quest support and the Touch controller profiles. XR Plug-in Management and OpenXR are already in the project's package list, so Unity downloads them once while the project first opens and this button takes seconds |
| **Multiplayer** | Opens the Photon setup window: 1) get a free Photon App ID, 2) paste it, 3) import Photon Fusion 2, then **Finish setup now** |
| **Build APK** | Switches to Android and builds `Builds/RagoExtract.apk` (asks to set up VR first if you haven't) |

### Multiplayer (Photon Fusion 2)
1. Press **Open Photon website**, make an account, **Create a new app** → **Fusion** → **Fusion 2**, and copy the App ID.
2. Paste it in the window.
3. Press **Import Photon Fusion 2**. If the SDK is already in your Downloads it's imported straight away; otherwise the Photon download page opens and the SDK is imported automatically as soon as the download finishes (or use **Pick .unitypackage...**).
4. Press **Finish setup now**. Multiplayer turns on after one recompile.

The Multiplayer window shows a checklist ([done] / [ todo ]) of each step, so you can see exactly what's left. In the game, the raid terminal in your bunker says ONLINE when it worked, or tells you exactly what's missing.

**Region:** everyone plays on one Photon region (US by default) so friends always land on the same servers. Players mostly in Europe or Asia: add a text file `Assets/RagoExtract/Resources/Rago/photon_region.txt` containing `eu` or `asia` (everyone must use the same build).

Online play runs on Photon's cloud in Shared mode: everyone meets in the Market, raids are matched per map (4-10 players, aims for 8, launches at 1:00), and the session's master client runs the raiders (it hands over automatically if they leave). Voice chat is built in: your microphone is streamed over the same connection and others hear you from your position in 3D. Without an App ID the game runs offline (solo raids with AI).

## How the game works

- **Bunker** is your private base. Everything you leave in it is saved automatically.
  - **Main hall**: the raid terminal, an **Exchange** sell counter next to the lift, and the lift to the Market.
  - **Armory** (through the north door): rifle rack, a pegboard and shelves for pistols, sights, mags, meds, loot and keys, a panel with your cash and ammo reserve, and a workbench.
  - **Armor Room** (through the east door): armor stands for helmets and vests, plus shelves for backpacks.
  - **Shooting range** (west tunnel) with hanging steel targets facing the firing line.

  Each stash section has a NEXT PAGE button when it's full.
- **First launch:** after a short LOADING screen you're in a dark registration room. Type your **callsign** on the terminal's keyboard (3-14 letters/numbers; on desktop, look at the screen and type) and press **CONFIRM** twice. The second press locks it in. **Your callsign can never be changed.** Every operator also has a 4-digit tag (for example REX#4821) so no two players ever show the same name; if two players with the same name and tag meet online, one of them is given a new tag automatically. Your callsign is shown on the steel nameplate in your bunker.
- **Training:** your first visit to the bunker starts a short training. Glowing markers and a line of text at the bottom of your view walk you through it: draw, shoot, reload, open your backpack, put on armor, see your stash and find the raid terminal. Your progress is saved, and **SKIP TRAINING** on the nameplate ends it.
- **Getting to the Market**: take the lift. In your bunker it's on the south wall (behind you when you spawn, under LIFT - MARKET). Press **CALL** on the panel next to it: the bell rings and the doors slide open. Walk in, and the doors close behind you and the lift goes up (or press GO UP inside). You arrive inside the Market's lift, which opens onto the Market. To go back, use the lift on the Market's east wall (LIFT - BUNKER) the same way.
- **Selling: the Exchange.** A big orange-and-black SELL counter in the middle of the Market plaza (right in front of you when you step out of the lift) and another in your bunker's main hall. Put anything in the bin on the counter, check the receipt (each item shows the best price any Market trader pays for it) and press **SELL ALL**. Anything you leave in the bin goes back to your stash, unsold. The traders' own SELL trays still work too.
- **Market layout**: a compact hall (32 x 24 m). Step out of the lift and the orange **SELL** counter (the Exchange) is right in front of you; the four traders stand in a row along the back wall to your right, each under a banner with their name; Bruno (bunker jobs) is on the far wall. Big lit signs hang from the roof over each (SELL, TRADERS, BUNKER JOBS), and a directory board by the lift doors points the way. There's a fire-barrel lounge in one corner.
- **Market** is a shared safe zone where players meet, with proximity voice chat. It has 4 NPC traders:
  - **Viktor** (rifles and shotguns)
  - **Mara** (sidearms, SMGs, holo sights, ammo, basic backpacks; also buys armor and bags)
  - **Doc Hale** (meds; also buys valuables)
  - **Silas** the Fence (buys **anything**, pays the best price for valuables, sells vault keycards)

  Each trader has a chalkboard price list: pull the paper **buy** tag next to an item and it appears on the PICKUP tray. Put items on the SELL tray to sell them. A backpack sells together with everything inside it.
- **Armor**: you start with none on. It's only found in raids: armor cases (olive boxes with a yellow stripe), crates, Raiders (heavies always wear a helmet and vest, and drop them), and vaults (Epic and Vault tiers). New profiles get a bump helmet and soft vest on the stands in the Armor Room. If you die and own no armor at all, a fresh basic set is waiting there.

  | Tier | Helmet (head damage stopped) | Vest (chest damage stopped, stomach 60% of that) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Common | Bump Helmet 15% | Soft Armor Vest 15% |
  | Uncommon | Ballistic Helmet 28% | Plate Carrier 30% |
  | Rare | FAST Rail Helmet 38% | Heavy Plate Carrier 42% |
  | Epic | Assault Visor Helmet 50% | Assault Armor Rig 52% |
  | Vault (vaults only) | Juggernaut Helmet 62% | Juggernaut Armor 65% |

  To wear armor, hold it to your head (helmet) or chest (vest) and let go. To take it off, grab the top of your head or your collar. Armor you wear is lost if you die.
- **Backpacks** are soft military rucksacks (like Ghost of Tabor) that you can see on players' and raiders' backs. Grab a bag (or pull yours off your left shoulder) and the flap flips back onto one big open compartment. Drop things in where you want them and they stack up: meds, loot, helmets, vests, pistols and SMGs. Things a bit too big get squeezed in; if something doesn't fit, it falls out and you're told why. Long guns go on the **rifle straps on the sides** of the bag (let go of the rifle next to the side).

  | Bag | Space | Rifle straps | Where |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Daypack | 14 L | 1 | Mara, raids |
  | Assault Pack | 26 L | 1 | Mara, raids |
  | Raid Pack | 39 L | 2 | raids (an SMG fits standing up) |
  | Tactical Pack | 53 L | 2 | raids |
  | Expedition Pack | 78 L | 2 | raids (rare), vaults |
  | Mule Hauler | 126 L | 2 | vaults only (even fits a rifle inside) |

  Swap bags by putting a different one on your left shoulder. A bag keeps everything in it and on its straps wherever it goes (stash, floor, trader, your body).
- **Meds**: Bandage (stops bleeding), Painkillers (quick +15), IFAK (+40 and stops bleeding), Field Medkit (+60), Trauma Kit (+90 and stops bleeding) and Adrenal Stim.
- **Loot to sell**: everything can be sold, but some loot exists only to be sold, from junk to jackpot:
  - **Junk** ($25-$300): tin can, scrap metal, cigarettes, Zippo lighter, battery pack.
  - **Decent** ($650-$2,200): whiskey, smartphone, silver ring, military radio, gold chain, luxury watch, encrypted drive.
  - **Good** ($4,500-$6,500): intel folder, graphics card, rugged laptop.
  - **Jackpot** ($9,000-$45,000): cold wallet and gold idol, plus the vault-only gold bar, blue diamond and Crown Jewel.

  Pick something up and its name, rarity and value pop up.
- **Bruno the Foreman** (Market, far wall under BUNKER JOBS) gives you **bunker expansion jobs**. Each job needs certain items plus a cash fee. Put the items on his TURN IN tray and press TURN IN ITEMS. When everything is in, press PAY & BUILD. The new room opens in your bunker; until then its doorway is a sealed, hazard-striped door.

  | # | Job | Bring | Fee | Unlocks |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | Clear the Storage Room | 5 Scrap Metal, 3 Tin Cans, 2 Battery Packs | $4,000 | **Storage Room** (off the Armory, west): two more racks with shelves |
  | 2 | Build a Med Bay | 3 Bandages, 1 IFAK, 2 Whiskey Bottles | $10,000 | **Med Bay** (off the Armor Room, south): a med cabinet that restocks meds after every raid |
  | 3 | Wire up a Generator | 4 Battery Packs, 2 Military Radios, 1 Graphics Card | $22,000 | **Generator Room** (off the Armory, east): earns $1,500 every raid (holds up to $15,000); press COLLECT |
  | 4 | Dig a Trophy Vault | 1 Rugged Laptop, 2 Luxury Watches, 1 Gold Idol | $50,000 | **Trophy Vault** (off the Armory, north): more armor stands, gun racks and a trophy plinth |

  Turned-in items are saved, so you can bring them over several raids.
- **Menu, friends and parties**: press the **LEFT MENU button** (the three lines; **N** on desktop) anywhere, in the bunker or in a raid. The menu opens in front of you:
  - **PARTY**: who's in your party, invites you've received (ACCEPT / DECLINE), LEAVE PARTY.
  - **FRIENDS**: **+ ADD FRIEND** and type their full callsign with the tag (like `GHOST#0193`), then INVITE or REMOVE.
  - **RECENT**: everyone in your current raid, then people you've raided with before: INVITE them or **+ FRIEND** them.
  - **SETTINGS**: grip angle, turning, volume, microphone (this replaces the old settings board).

  Your first invite starts a party with you as the leader. Invites sent from your bunker reach friends who are in their bunkers; invites sent in a raid reach people in that raid, and you team up when you're both back in your bunkers. Everyone stays in their own bunker; the **leader** picks a map and presses QUEUE on the raid terminal and every member's terminal follows. The whole party goes into the same raid (random players can still fill the free spots) and **spawns together**, side by side. In the raid, teammates have a **green dot** over their heads that you can see through walls. The raid waits for the whole party (still launching at 1:00 at the latest), and if the leader cancels, everyone's queue cancels. Parties need multiplayer set up (Rago Extract > Multiplayer).
- **Party screen**: a small green screen beside the raid terminal's QUEUE key shows your party - every member, who leads, and whether QUEUE takes the whole party (leader) or you follow your leader in (member). When the leader queues, every member's terminal queues too and everyone gets the FINDING RAID timer.
- **Launch at 1:00**: the minute counts from when you pressed QUEUE, and the raid launches when it gets there - if the session host's go never reaches you, your own game takes you in a few seconds later, into the same raid.
- **Players online**: the raid terminal's header shows how many people are playing right now ("12 PLAYERS ONLINE"), refreshed every couple of seconds. It's Photon's own count of everyone connected to the game - in their bunkers, the Market, parties and raids - or, if your Photon SDK doesn't expose that, everyone in a Market / party / raid session plus you. Offline it says OFFLINE.
- **Raid terminal map**: the raid terminal's screen is a big map of the three islands and the hatch down to the Underground Sector. Point at a location and pull the trigger (click on desktop) to pick it - a ring marks your choice and the bottom line shows its player limits - then press QUEUE on the console below.
- **Underground Sector**: a sealed research complex **three levels deep** (B1, B2, B3), each a grid of rooms (offices, storage, labs, barracks, server rooms, generator halls, armories) joined by dim service corridors and stacked over each other. Two wide (2.2 m) stairwells link each level to the next: a railed opening in the floor above, and a stair in a walled shaft below ("DOWN TO B2" / "UP TO B1" signs; LEVEL markers on the corridor walls). **Two vaults** (one on B2, one on B3) and only **two extracts**: the Freight Elevator in the middle of B1 and the Drainage Tunnel in a corner of B3. Armories, server rooms, generator halls and barracks are often held by bigger groups of raiders (up to 22 per raid here). **6 players per raid, squads of up to 2**, all starting round the edge of B1.
- **Raider voices** carry about 30 m for shouts and 16 m for talk, fading to silence, and are muffled and much quieter through walls. Raiders shout - when they spot you ("Contact!"), push or flank, reload, get hit, lose you ("Where did he go?"), hear something, get flashed, drop you, or see a mate go down - and chat now and then on patrol. Each raider keeps one of three voices, and everyone in the raid hears the same lines.
- **Keycards**: besides safes, a few Vault Keycards lie loose around every raid map (usually on desks) - at least one per vault, in different places each raid.
- **Crates face the room**: crates and cases sit squared up against the nearest wall with the lid opening towards you; weapon cases and duffels lie lengthways along desks and walls.
- **Hands** line up with your real hands: the fingers point where the controller points (from its aim pose), the same angle guns are held at.
- **Holding things**: guns point where your controller points (the game reads the controller's aim pose, so pistols and rifles sit in your hand at the right angle on any headset); the Grip angle setting still fine-tunes it. Magazines sit in your fist like a real mag - top up, rounds forward, ready to push into the magwell - and grenades fuse-up; other small things (meds, sights, keycards, valuables) are held with their long side through your fist; big ones (helmets, vests, bags) are held where you grabbed them.
- **No wall tricks**: lean or climb your head into a wall or roof and your view goes black; a gun barrel poked through a wall shoots the wall; climbing can't pop you onto a roof, and on indoor maps you're put back if you ever get out under the open sky.
- **Bleeding**: big hits can start you bleeding. A bandage or medkit stops it at once (hold it near your chest or head and pull the trigger). Without meds it's a gamble: every few seconds a wound may clot by itself - light bleeds usually do, heavy ones rarely - so you might make it or bleed out. Standing still helps, sprinting doesn't, and pressing an empty hand on your chest (hold grip there) halves the blood loss and makes it clot much sooner.
- **Grenades and flashbangs on raiders**: a frag's shrapnel hits whatever part of them is nearest the blast (so a vest doesn't save them from a grenade at their feet) and kills an ordinary raider within about 3 m. A flashbang blinds raiders who can see it: they stop, lose track of you and can't shoot until it wears off.
- **Bosses**: every raid map has a named boss who turns up in roughly one raid in four, in their lair with three heavily armed guards - **The Harbormaster** (Dockyard, in the harbor office), **The Warden** (Pinewood, in the command bunker), **The Banker** (Old Town, in the bank) and **Doctor Kessler** (Underground, in front of the B3 vault). Each boss has **their own gun and armour that nothing else in the game has** (Rarity: Boss, red): Kessler carries **Kessler's Lancet** (chrome, integrally suppressed 5.7 SMG) with a white Hazard Visor and Lab Plate; the Harbormaster **Harbormaster's Anchor** (full-auto drum shotgun) with a rust-orange Dive Helm and Deck Armor; the Warden **Warden's Longbow** (.338 bolt-action) with a green Ranger Helmet and Field Rig; the Banker the **Golden Parachute** (gold 7.62 bullpup) with a Gilded Helmet and Gold Plate. Kill the boss and the gun, helmet and vest drop with the body, plus a Vault Keycard and vault-grade loot. Bosses take a lot to put down, aim better and stay near their lair.
- **Vaults fit their buildings**: the Dockyard and Pinewood vaults are annexes built in the same walls (material, thickness and height) as the building they're attached to; the Underground vaults run across the whole back of their room, using the room's own walls.
- **Raiders** see you properly (their shots hit as before), patrol more (a new spot every 12-25 s, further afield), sometimes roam towards where the players are, and come to check gunshots within about 45 m.
- **Racks and shelves**: bring something to a stash rack, shelf, armour stand or your holsters and a small green marker shows the spot it'll go to - let go and it's stored. Rifles hang anywhere up a rifle-rack column, and every spot judges from the middle of what you're holding.
- **Your gear stays on your body**: belt pouches, holsters, the slung rifle and your bag hang from your neck, so looking down at them doesn't swing them up in front of your face.
- **Doors**: ordinary doorways in the raid maps have doors. Grab one anywhere with the grip button and swing it with your hand, or just walk into it or shove it - a push swings it open on its own. Each door swings one way - into the roomier side (the room, not the corridor), so doors facing each other never jam - and stays where it ends up. Doors are solid: they stop against walls, furniture and other doors. Raiders and other players push them too (raiders path straight through doorways and shove the door as they come). Some doors are already open when the raid starts. Vault entrances, gates and wide openings have no door.
- **Clear doorways**: the world builder remembers every doorway it cuts, and shelves, tables, crates and loot never block one or the space just in front of or behind it.
- **Loot spots**: every map's layout is the same each raid (so you can learn it), but which crates appear - and what's in them - changes every raid. Crates sit on the floor against a wall or on a desk/bench; never on stairs or in the middle of nowhere.
- **Raid terminal** (in your bunker, east wall; there are no raid terminals in the Market): pick a map on the green screen and press the big **QUEUE** key. Each raid holds 4-10 players. The queue tries to reach 8, starts right away at 10, and once the queue timer hits **1 minute** it launches with whoever is queued. While you're queued, the top-left of your view shows **FINDING RAID** with a timer, then **RAID FOUND** and **JOINING RAID** (with its own timer, which runs until you are in the raid) when a match is ready.
- **Raids**: three maps.
  - **Dockyard**: container yard, cranes, warehouses, Harbor Office vault, and the **Workers' Village** through the west gate.
  - **Pinewood Outpost**: forest, military outpost, bunker vault, and the **North Hamlet** of wooden houses past the forest.
  - **Old Town**: streets, apartments, bank vault, and the **Suburbs** at the north end of the main street.

  **Every map is a 1 km island.** The land is surrounded by an irregular coastline: grass gives way to sandy beaches (wet sand at the waterline) that slope into open sea running to the horizon, with rocks, driftwood and old wooden jetties along the shore. You can wade into the shallows up to the edge of the map; the open sea won't drown you (the Dockyard's harbour water still will). Around each town there's open country with roads out to far-off locations: farms (barn, farmhouse, silos), army camps (tents, sandbags, a watchtower, weapon/armor cases), gas stations, warehouses, cabin clusters and little streets. Each has loot and raiders. There are **3 extracts per map**: one near the town and two far out on opposite sides. A border fence surrounds it all.

  **Spawns:** you start out near the border, with every player in a raid at a different spot (10 per map, 6 on Dockyard because of the sea). About half are small lootable places: a tool shed, a hunter's cabin, a supply tent or an open shipping container, each with a few crates to search before you move out. The rest are out in the open behind a wrecked car, a rock pile or an old sandbag nest. No raider squad starts within 110 m of a spawn.

  **Field map:** press **Y** (VR, when your left hand isn't holding a gun) or **M** (desktop). It's a real top-down photo of the map taken when the raid starts, printed on paper, with every extract circled in green and a red arrow showing where you are and which way you're facing. In VR it floats just above your left hand, turned toward your eyes.

  The village, hamlet and suburbs are streets of houses you can walk into and search room by room (stairs, furniture, loot crates inside), and each has its own extract.

  Each raid lasts 40 minutes. To leave, reach an extract (green smoke), step into the zone and use the **laptop** on the supply crate (grab it, click its CALL key, or press F on desktop). A **10-second** countdown runs on the laptop and your HUD. **Extracts are locked at the start:** each one opens at its own random time between 5 and 10 minutes into the raid (the same for everyone in the raid). A locked extract has a red light and no smoke, its laptop counts down to when it opens, and the field map shows when each one opens. Stay in the zone until it hits zero; leaving the zone cancels the call. If you die, you lose what you brought and your body drops it for others to loot.
- **Grenades, flashbangs and knives** (found in crates and on raiders, and sold by Mara the Quartermaster; they fit your belt pouches):
  - **M67 Frag Grenade**: hold it, pull the **trigger** to pull the pin (click, the ring comes off), then throw. It goes off 3.6 s after the pin is pulled, wherever it is - even in your hand. Deadly within a few metres, hurts out to about 9 m; walls and floors block it.
  - **M84 Flashbang**: same, with a 2.2 s fuse. Anyone with a line of sight to it is blinded (white-out) with ringing ears - worst if you're looking at it. Raiders who see it can't see or shoot for a few seconds and stagger about.
  - **Combat Knife / Hunting Knife**: swing or stab. The faster the blade moves, the harder it hits; head hits hurt most.
  - Desktop: click to pull the pin, click again to throw where you're looking; click with a knife to stab what you're looking at.
- **Raiders**: up to 16 per raid, spread over the whole map in small squads. They patrol slowly, moving to a new spot about every 30 seconds, notice you over time when they can see you, take cover, peek, fire bursts with human-like aim, flinch when hit, ragdoll on death and drop their gun and loot. A gunshot only brings raiders over if they're close (about 30 m); farther away they just stop and look toward the sound, and only come for you if they spot you. Footsteps are very quiet: walking, a raider only hears you if you're right next to them (about 2 m), sprinting about 5 m, and crouch-walking makes no sound. A vault alarm pulls in everyone nearby.
- **Woods**: pines with layered needle branches, leafy oaks and birches, bushes you can walk through and hide in, and patches of tall grass and ferns, all swaying in the wind. Raids have no draw-distance limit, only a light haze, so you can see across the whole map.
- **Guns**: 50 original guns (pistols, revolvers, SMGs, shotguns, rifles, DMRs, snipers, LMGs) in 5 rarity tiers.
  - Common ones turn up everywhere and Rare/Epic ones are hard to find.
  - 7 **Vault** guns spawn only inside vaults. Open a vault with a Vault Keycard: hold it to the card reader next to the vault door. Keycards are rare loot, and safes often have one.
- **Bolt-action rifles** (9): Volga Bolt, Marsh M44 and Ranger Scout (iron sights), Bolt Hunter 700, Prairie Varmint (5.56), Polar AW, Harrier LR and Kodiak (.338) with rails for optics, plus the Leviathan .50. Fire, then grab the bolt handle with your other hand and pull it back and forward to load the next round.
- **Iron sights**: every gun's irons are a real notch-and-post: a rear sight with a gap in it and a thin front post between two guard ears, with glowing green dots (three-dot on pistols). Put the front post in the middle of the rear gap with its top level with the tops of the rear sight, and the shot goes where the post is.
- **Attachments** (optics boxes, crates, vaults, and Mara sells the common ones): bring one to the right spot on a gun and let go to mount it, grab it to take it off. **Muzzle**: Compact Suppressor, Long Suppressor (quiet shots, tiny flash, raiders only hear you close up), Muzzle Brake, Tri-Port Compensator (less recoil). **Side rail** (under the frame on pistols): Weapon Light, Red Laser, Green Laser, PEQ Light + Laser - on while you hold the gun. **Under the handguard** (long guns): Vertical, Angled and Stubby grips (less recoil). They stay on the gun in your stash.
- **Magnified scopes**: Combat 3x Prism (red chevron), Hunter 4x Scope (duplex crosshair) and Longshot 8x Scope (mil-dot with a red centre dot). They mount on any top rail like a holo. Put your eye up to the eyepiece (a few cm back) and you see a real magnified picture - the scope has its own camera, and its zoom is worked out from how big the eyepiece looks to you, so 4x is always 4x. Like a real scope it has an eye box: off-axis or too far back and a black ring closes in. The scope only renders while you're holding the gun and looking through it.
- **Zeroed sights**: shots go where the sights point - the bullet crosses the line of sight at 25 m for rifle irons (15 m pistols), 50 m for red dots and holos, 100 m for scopes.
- **Holo sights** (6 types) are found in optics boxes. Reticles are bigger and bolder so they read in a headset. Bring a sight to a gun's top rail and let go to mount it. The reticle is projected at infinity, like a real holo sight.
- **Effects**: muzzle flash with light, muzzle smoke, ejected brass, tracers, sparks, dust and debris, and bullet holes. Blood comes as spray, mist, wound decals, splatter on walls and floors, drips while bleeding, and pools under bodies.
- **Sound**: every sound is synthesized in code. This includes layered gunshots with echo tails, suppressed shots, reload mechanics, footsteps per surface, impacts, bullet cracks, alarms and ambience.

### VR controls
| | |
|---|---|
| Left stick | Move. Click to sprint (uses stamina) |
| A / X | Jump (always, gun in hand or not) |
| Left MENU button (three lines) | Open / close the menu: party, friends, recent players, settings |
| Y (left hand not holding a gun) | Open / close the field map (in raids) |
| Right stick | Snap or smooth turn. Click to crouch toggle (real crouching also works) |
| Grip | Grab, two-hand a gun (grab the handguard), pull a slide or charging handle |
| Grip while pointing an empty hand at something up to 3.5 m away | Distance grab: the item glows when you point at it; squeeze and it flies into your hand. Point at a closed crate and squeeze to open it |
| Grip on a wall, ledge, ladder, fence or rock (empty hand) | Climb: the hand locks on and pulling down lifts you. Let go above a ledge to climb onto it |
| Yellow AMMO pouch at either hip | Grip there (gun in the other hand) to pull a fresh magazine for that gun. Let go of a mag or a box of ammo you found there to store it - the flap turns green when it'll go in. Hand near the pouch shows your reserve for the guns you carry |
| Trigger | Fire. Point at buttons and pull the trigger to click |
| B / Y (gun hand) | Tap: drop the magazine. Hold half a second: switch fire mode (semi / auto / burst) |
| Other hand on the slide / charging handle / bolt | Hold TRIGGER (or grip) on the handle itself (the pistol slide, or the rifle's charging handle / bolt on its top or side - not by the trigger) and pull back, then let it go forward, to rack (chamber a round, release a locked slide) |
| Right hip | Pistol holster (with a thigh strap) |
| Front of stomach | Front sling: your main long gun hangs low across your stomach, muzzle down to the left, well below your face - works without a backpack. Let go of a rifle in front of your stomach to sling it |
| Lower back | Second long gun |
| Left shoulder | Backpack: pull it off and it opens. Let go of things inside it to pack them, or next to its sides to strap a rifle on |
| Top of head / collar (upper chest) | Grip to take off your helmet / vest. Hold armor to your head or chest and let go to wear it |

### Desktop controls (no headset)

## Code layout
```
Assets/RagoExtract/
  Runtime/   core game (no package dependencies; always compiles)
    Art/     procedural mesh kit, materials/textures, gun, character and sight builders
    Audio/   sound synthesis + pooled 3D playback
    Fx/      muzzle flash, tracers, brass, impacts, decals, blood
    Items/   item database (58 guns, sights, attachments, meds, loot), guns, mags, containers
    Player/  XR input, physics hands, locomotion, ragdoll body, gear, vitals
    AI/      raid NPCs + squad director
    World/   level builder, raid maps, bunker, market, crates, vaults, extraction
    Hub/     kiosk / matchmaking UI, traders, stash, settings
    Net/     transport-agnostic replication (players, shots, hits, loot, NPCs)
  Fusion/    Photon Fusion 2 backend (compiles once Fusion is imported and multiplayer is switched on)
  Editor/    first-open setup + the Rago Extract tab (Set Up VR / Multiplayer / Build APK)
  Shaders/   lit (triplanar), unlit FX, holo reticle, sky
```
If the Photon module ever fails to compile against your Fusion version, the editor switches it off so the game keeps running offline; pressing **Finish setup now** tries again.
